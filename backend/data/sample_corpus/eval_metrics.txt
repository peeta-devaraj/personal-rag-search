Evaluating a retrieval system requires more than checking whether the top result looks reasonable; it requires a labeled set of queries with known relevant documents, and quantitative metrics computed against that set. Precision at k measures the fraction of the top k retrieved documents that are actually relevant, while recall at k measures the fraction of all relevant documents that were successfully retrieved within the top k results.

Mean reciprocal rank rewards a system for placing the first relevant result as high as possible, computing the reciprocal of the rank of the first relevant hit and averaging across queries. Normalized discounted cumulative gain, or nDCG, generalizes this further by supporting graded relevance judgments rather than a simple binary relevant-or-not label, and discounts the contribution of relevant documents that appear lower in the ranked list using a logarithmic decay.
