An inverted index is the core data structure behind most sparse text search engines. Instead of scanning every document for a query term, the system maintains a mapping from each term in the vocabulary to a "posting list" of the documents (and often positions within those documents) where that term occurs. Looking up a query term becomes a fast dictionary lookup followed by a scan of a much smaller posting list, rather than a linear scan of the entire corpus.

Building an inverted index typically involves tokenizing each document, normalizing tokens (lowercasing, stemming, or lemmatizing), removing common stopwords, and then aggregating term occurrences across the corpus. Compression techniques such as delta encoding of document IDs within a posting list, combined with variable-byte or Elias-gamma encoding, make it feasible to store inverted indexes for web-scale corpora in a fraction of the space a naive representation would require, while still supporting fast intersection and union operations needed to answer boolean and ranked queries.
