PageRank is a link analysis algorithm originally developed by Larry Page and Sergey Brin to rank web pages in Google's search results. It models a random surfer who follows hyperlinks between pages and occasionally jumps to a random page, and computes each page's importance as the stationary probability of the surfer being on that page. Pages linked to by many other important pages receive a higher PageRank score. Unlike pure term-matching approaches, PageRank captures a notion of authority that is independent of the query, and is typically combined with content-based relevance signals such as TF-IDF or BM25 scores in a full ranking pipeline.

The damping factor in PageRank, usually set to about 0.85, represents the probability that the random surfer continues clicking links rather than jumping to an arbitrary page. This factor ensures the algorithm converges and prevents rank sinks, which are pages or groups of pages with no outbound links that would otherwise absorb all the rank in the graph.
